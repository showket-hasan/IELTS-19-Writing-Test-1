Task 2 Instructions & Prompt Guidelines

40 Minutes Recommended
Official Examination Essay Topic

You should spend about 40 minutes on this task.

Write about the following topic:

Some people think that competition at work, at school and in daily life is a good thing. Others believe that we should try to cooperate more, rather than competing against each other.

Discuss both these views and give your own opinion.

Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience.

Write at least 250 words.

Essential Essay Criteria & Structural Checklist:

Component Official Expectation Cambridge 19 Pitfall to Avoid
View 1: Competition Analyze benefits: drive, meritocracy, innovation, high standards in school/work. Do not merely praise cooperation while neglecting deep analysis of competition.
View 2: Cooperation Analyze benefits: team synergy, reduced toxic stress, collective problem solving. Avoid superficial anecdotes without broad analytical rationale.
Personal Opinion State clear nuanced position throughout (e.g. balanced synthesis). CRITICAL: Avoid contradictions (e.g. concluding "life is a competition, be a winner" after arguing competition is destructive).

Cambridge Band 9.0 Exemplar Model Essay for Task 2
In contemporary society, whether human endeavors in professional, academic, and personal spheres should be driven by competition or anchored in cooperation remains a contentious debate. While advocates of competition argue that it fuels motivation and excellence, proponents of cooperation contend that collective harmony and shared effort yield superior outcomes. In my view, although healthy competition serves as a potent catalyst for individual drive, cooperative synergy is fundamentally more indispensable for sustainable long-term success. On the one hand, competition offers undeniable benefits by incentivizing individuals to reach their maximum potential. In academic environments, competitive benchmarking encourages pupils to cultivate discipline and master demanding curricula. Similarly, in commercial and workplace settings, rivalry among professionals and corporate entities spurs innovation, refines operational efficiency, and ensures that merit is rewarded. When individuals strive to surpass their peers, complacency is minimized, prompting continuous self-improvement and resilience. On the other hand, cooperation fosters psychological well-being and collective problem-solving that solitary competition cannot replicate. Complex modern challenges—such as technological innovation, healthcare delivery, and global environmental sustainability—require multidisciplinary teamwork rather than individualistic confrontation. Excessive competition frequently engenders a cut-throat atmosphere characterized by chronic anxiety, burnout, and distrust. By contrast, a collaborative ethos promotes psychological safety, mutual empathy, and knowledge sharing, enabling groups to achieve synergistic solutions that far exceed individual capabilities. In conclusion, while competition certainly plays a constructive role in stimulating ambition and high performance, an unbridled competitive culture risks eroding social cohesion and psychological health. I firmly believe that fostering deep cooperation must serve as the primary cornerstone of modern education and the workplace, with healthy competition reserved merely as a secondary motivating factor.
